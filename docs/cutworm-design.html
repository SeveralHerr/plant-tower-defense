Pest file — boss no. 3The Cutworm

A single body that occupies fifteen road cells at once. It comes out of the west burrow head first and keeps coming; by the time the head is a third of the way down the road the tail has not arrived yet. It is the only pest in its wave, because it is the wave — and it is the first pest whose answer is not at the front of the queue.

The thing to judge

It has to read as a worm through the corners

The road turns five times. Every other pest is a 64 px sprite that snaps to one of four cardinal rotations at a corner and nobody notices. A 960 px body cannot do that — it is inside three corners at once. Drive it below and watch the bends.

head at cell 0 · 22 stations

Naive sprite chain is what falls out of copying Pest: fifteen 64 px sprites spaced one cell apart, each snapped to a cardinal rotation. Turn it on at a corner. The outside of the turn opens gaps between the sprites, the inside stacks them into a lump, and every segment is square to the screen while the body is diagonal. It reads as a train, not an animal.

Mechanism — in order

Four things, and only the fourth is decoration

The body is not a chain of followers. It is a continuous outline swept along the road itself, so it cannot drift off the dirt, cannot open a gap, and cannot disagree with the route the head is walking.

01 — SPINE

Fillet the route's corners

Board.route() is one waypoint per cell, so its corners are exact right angles. Cut each corner back by r = 26 px and replace the vertex with a quadratic Bézier through it. The spine is now tangent-continuous everywhere.

Measured, not assumed: swept over the whole walk at 26 px fillet and 29 px girth, 0 of 104,136 body-edge samples leave a road cell. The fillet cuts toward the corner cell, which is dirt, so the bend costs no grass. It does shorten the spine — 2112 px becomes 2053, −2.8% — so progress() and the walk clock must stay keyed to the unfilleted route or every wave-timing number in the file moves under the boss.

02 — STATIONS

Park the body by arc length, not by following

One scalar of state: head_s, the head's distance along the spine. Station i sits at head_s − i × 44. No position history buffer, no per-segment lerp, nothing to desynchronise, and the whole worm is one save-able number.

Before head_s reaches i × 44 the station has not emerged; the body is simply cut off at s = 0, which is off the west edge of the board. That is the burrow, for free.

03 — OUTLINE

Sweep a varying half-width down the spine

Every 4 px along the live span, take the normal and offset ±radius(u). Left edge forward, right edge back, close, fill, stroke the rim. The segmentation is drawn on top as ticks across the body at each station — annelid rings, not sprite seams.

radius(u) is a blunt head, a long even trunk, and a tail that tapers to a point over the last quarter. That taper is what makes it read head-and-tail rather than tube.

04 — PERISTALSIS

Wave the girth, not the spine

A 64 px lane leaves no room to slither sideways, and a snake wiggle would put the body on the grass. Real earthworms don't wiggle anyway — they move by a thickness wave running head to tail. Modulate radius by ±10% at a 150 px wavelength and the same term surges the head speed ±25%.

One sine, two readers. The body pulses, the worm lurches, and both are the same motion, so they can never look out of step with each other. 10% is the largest swing the lane affords: 29 × 1.10 = 31.9 against a 32 px half-lane.

Art

Pale grub, red mouth — measured, not picked

The obvious choice is the pest-red family every other bug wears. It fails here for a reason that only shows up at this size: the road is #BB8044, and #E74C3C on it is 1.14:1. A 64 px aphid gets away with that because it is small and moving; 960 px of it is a red-brown smear on a red-brown road.

Sand/paper — the kit's own #ECDCB8 — is 2.47:1 on the same dirt, and a pale grub is what a cutworm actually looks like. The red stays where it earns its keep: the maw, and the one band you have to shoot.

The fight

The first pest answered from behind

Fifteen cells of body means every plant on the board is in range of something at once. Left alone that is 60+ DPS against a single target and the boss evaporates — a level-3 Corn Cobbler that lands one kernel of five on an aphid lands all five on a wall. So the hide has to matter, and where you may hurt it is the whole design.

ZoneDamage takenWhereWhy
Maw 100% the head Shooting the face works, and it is what every player tries first. It is also the part that is eating your garden, so it is the honest option and never the efficient one.
Hide 15% the trunk Not 0. A player raking the body should see chip damage, not blanks — blanks read as a bug. 15% keeps a full board honest without letting it brute-force the fight.
Weak band 250% stations 5–7, ~2 cells The clitellum. It rides five stations behind the head, which is 220 px of road the head has already walked — and already chewed.

That last row is the fight. The band arrives where the head has just been, so the plants that can hit it are the plants the worm has just eaten past. You are not defending a line in front of it; you are building a kill box behind it and paying for the ground with plants you already know you will lose.

  • It cannot be heldA Chomp Flower's mouth refuses it outright — chew_seconds is meaningless against a body that never ends. The Chomp flinches and stays shut. One boss trivialised by one 50-seed plant is not a boss.
  • It cannot be blockedA Barrier Bramble does not stop it. The head eats through at EAT_DPS 40 — roughly three times an ordinary pest — and keeps its speed. Brambles buy the garden nothing here, which is exactly the wave where that is worth learning.
  • It can be slowedSticky Sundew still bites, at half effect. Slowing it lengthens the window your kill box has on the band, so the answer to a boss you cannot block is a boss you can stretch.
  • It escapes tail-lastThe wave is not over when the head leaves the board. Damage keeps landing on the band and the tail for another 48 s, and the lives are only spent when the tail clears. The comeback is real and the tension holds to the end.
  • It is aloneOne row, one entry: {"species": &"cutworm", "count": 1, "gap": 1.0, "lead": 0.5}. Nothing else spawns. WaveDirector.SIMULTANEOUS_PEST_CEILING is untouched — one pest node, whatever its length.

speed20 px/s Below the Locust's 24, so it is now the slowest thing that has ever walked this road — and it is both the slow one and the long one. 2112 px ÷ 20 = 105.6 s head-to-exit, and the tail clears 953 px later at 153.3 s. Surges between 15 and 25 on the peristaltic term.
peristalsis±10% Not a taste call. 29 × 1.10 = 31.9, a tenth of a pixel inside the 32 px lane half-width; at ±12% the fat phase of the wave puts 5,568 edge samples on the grass. The amplitude is whatever the lane has left over after the girth.
seeds120 Three Aphid Queens (40 each). It is the last wave's payout and it has to fund the rebuild of everything the head ate.
stations × spacing22 × 44 px 924 px between end stations plus the 29 px head cap = 953 px, which is 14.9 of the road's 32 cells. 44 is well under the 58 px body width, so the ring ticks read as rings rather than as joints.
girth29 px half-width 58 px of body in a 64 px lane — 3 px of dirt showing each side. The hard ceiling is 32 (half a cell) and it is set by the straights, not the corners: swept at girth 33 the body clips grass, at 32 it does not. 29 is 32 minus the peristaltic swing below.
Every one of these is an opening bid. Wave tables, prices and reach in this repo are tuned through test/unit/test_playtest_sweep.gd, and a boss that changes what a full board's DPS is worth will move numbers outside its own species row. The health figure above is the one most likely to be wrong.
Build

What it touches

A Cutworm extends Pest, not a seventh entry in SPECIES pretending nothing is different. It keeps the health pool, the damage pipeline, the death signal, the "pests" group and progress() — everything targeting already reads — and replaces exactly two things: how it moves, and how it draws.

FileChange
game/cutworm.gdnew. Owns head_s, the filleted spine, _draw() of the swept outline, and the zone lookup that turns a hit position into a 0.15 / 1.0 / 2.5 multiplier.
game/pest.gdOne SPECIES row with "boss": true so wave_carries_boss() and the drought rule pick it up for free. take_damage() grows an optional hit-position argument; every existing caller keeps working without it.
game/board.gdExpose the filleted spine beside route(). Nothing else on the board changes — same corners, same 32 cells, same 2112 px.

Known risks, before anyone writes a line. Corn Cobbler targeting picks the pest furthest along the road — with one pest on the board that is trivially the worm, so the whole board fires at it and the hide multiplier is the only thing standing between this and a two-second boss. The health bar wants rethinking too: a 32 px bar over a 960 px body is a joke, and it probably belongs on the HUD as a boss bar. And findings' orphan growth check watches node counts — the swept outline adds none, which is a point in its favour over a 22-sprite chain.
